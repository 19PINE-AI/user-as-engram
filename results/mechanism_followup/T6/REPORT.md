# T6: Is a neuron a rank-1 LoRA? Gated neuron vs rank-1 LoRA vs ROME for storing facts (Qwen3-8B)

**Status (2026-11-10): complete.** All GPU work finished 2026-11-09 22:32.
- Single-fact sweep: 5 methods × 9 layers × 200 facts unregularised, plus Q/K/V LoRA.
- +reg single-fact at **L7, L15, L23 only** (plus a partial L3), and Q/K/V+reg.
- Multi-fact (10 and 50 facts per edit) at L7/L15/L23 unregularised; **+reg multi-fact at L15 only**.
- Supplementary other-subject firing at L15.
- Scope cuts were made by the lead/user. Raw data: runs*.jsonl (12 shards, no duplicate keys).

## 1. Question and design

- **Question.** Is a neuron functionally like a rank-1 LoRA when used to store a fact? Specifically:
  - Does a gate, on its own, give the locality that User-as-Engram gets by addressing?
  - Does any rank-1 edit compose (two-hop)?
  - Where in depth does storage work?
- **Facts.** 200 fictional people. Each fact is "<name> lives in the city of <real city>". Cities are kept only if
  the base model already knows city→country (55/60 cities).
- **Training.** Each edit stores ONE fact at ONE layer. It is trained on the direct template with 5 prefixes:
  ≤100 Adam steps, early stop at target NLL < 0.05. LR is chosen once on 5 dev facts at L19: 1e-3 for all
  single-unit methods, 1e-4 for Q/K/V.
- **Methods.**
  - `lora_down`: rank-1 ΔW on down_proj, ungated.
  - `lora_up`: rank-1 ΔW on up_proj. Its output passes through the existing SiLU gates.
  - `neuron`: one new SwiGLU unit (gate row + up row + down column) appended to the MLP. This is exactly
    "a neuron".
  - `neuron_bias`: the same unit with a trainable gate bias.
  - `rome`: closed-form rank-1 edit keyed on the subject's covariance-whitened MLP input.
  - `qkv`: rank-16 LoRA on Q/K/V at every layer (the repo-style per-user LoRA).
  - `+reg`: the same training plus KL(base‖edit) on anchors: 16 other people with the same relation, 8
    other-relation prompts, and 2×128 tokens of generic text. These are the counter-examples. Training runs a fixed
    100 steps.
- **Metrics.** Mean over 200 facts with 95% bootstrap CIs over facts.
  - direct, para (3 held-out templates) and two-hop (2 templates asking for the *country*, never trained):
    greedy full-target accuracy.
  - **bleed**: probability of the edited city on 40 *other* people with the same relation. With no edit it is
    4.6e-4.
  - **fire10**: fraction of wikitext tokens where the edit's unit exceeds 10% of its activation on the fact
    prompt.
  - **resid-move**: fraction of wikitext tokens whose residual at the edited layer moves >1%.
  - text KL/tok: on 4,096 wikitext-103 test tokens per edit.
  - neighbor KL: same person, other relations.
- **References.** Base model: 0 on every metric. Fact stated in context (ICL): para 0.66, two-hop 0.52.
- **Measurement floor.** A zero edit gives exactly 0 on every KL/residual metric, because base and edited passes
  use identical batch shapes.

## 2. Answers

1. **A gate makes the unit sparse on unrelated text, but not specific to the subject.**
   - Unregularised, the new neuron fires on 0.5–5% of wikitext tokens at L7–L31. The rank-1 down_proj LoRA's
     "unit" fires on 0.2–94%.
   - The neuron moves the residual on 13–46% of tokens vs 71–99% for LoRA at L11–L35.
   - But it leaks the fact to other people as much as LoRA: bleed 0.35–0.72 at L7–L23 vs 0.09–0.79.
   - Without pressure, the gate keys on the relation phrase, not the person.
   - **The gate alone does not reproduce Engram's locality** (Engram: exactly 0.000 off-trigger).
2. **Counter-examples (+reg) make every method subject-specific, and the gate isn't needed for it.**
   - Bleed falls 20–200×. neuron+reg: 0.005 / 0.015 / 0.035 at L7 / L15 / L23. lora_down+reg: 0.003 / 0.004 /
     0.076. lora_up+reg: 0.003 / 0.004 / 0.085. qkv+reg: 0.016 (vs 0.50 unregularised).
   - Rank-1 LoRA is as specific as the gated neuron, or more so at L15.
   - The neuron's text sparsity is unchanged by +reg (fire10 0.027 / 0.013 / 0.068).
   - So the neuron's distinctive property is *sparsity*, not *addressing*.
3. **Specificity is free early and costly in the middle.**
   - At L7, +reg keeps or improves paraphrase and leaves two-hop unchanged (neuron 0.27, LoRA 0.33).
   - At L15 and L23, +reg halves two-hop or worse: lora_down 0.39→0.13 and 0.36→0.04; neuron 0.40→0.20 and
     0.32→0.09.
   - ROME, keyed on the subject by construction, is still the best single edit at L7: bleed 0.003, para 0.59,
     two-hop 0.49, against an ICL ceiling of 0.52.
   - Cost of every subject-specific edit (ROME, +reg): other facts about *the same person* shift more. Neighbor KL
     is 1.3–1.7 vs about 1.0.
4. **Storage near the output gives recall but not composition. This is the essay's "near the output" claim, and
   it holds sharply.**
   - Two-hop peaks at L15–L19 (ROME: L11, 0.61), is falling at L23, and collapses at L27 for *every* method:
     0.02–0.05. It is **0.000 at L31/L35**, while direct recall stays ≈1.0 and paraphrase stays 0.33–0.68.
   - So a fact written in the last ~quarter of the network (L27–35 of 36) is retrievable verbatim but not usable
     for one more hop.
   - Rank-1 edits at L7–L19 do compose (0.27–0.61, vs about 13% for User-as-Engram's chained pairs). Caveat: here
     the second hop is knowledge the model already has.
5. **ROME at the last layer (L35) has no effect at all** (direct 0.000). This is structural, not a failure. ROME
   writes at the subject's last token, and after the final layer no attention can carry it to the answer position.
6. **A gate bias adds nothing**: neuron_bias ≈ neuron on every metric.

**Answer to "are neurons like rank-1 LoRA?"**
- As a fact store, yes. A gated neuron and a rank-1 LoRA reach the same recall, paraphrase and two-hop, depth for
  depth (mostly within ±0.06).
- With counter-examples, both become equally subject-specific.
- What the gate adds is sparsity: the unit stays silent on most unrelated tokens.
- What neither gets without a subject-keyed input (ROME, Engram's hash address) or explicit counter-examples is
  addressing.

## 3. Methods notes and caveats

- Edits are batched: E independent edits share a forward pass, with rows routed to their own parameters. Adam is
  element-wise, so this matches one-at-a-time training. Verified: same step counts and near-equal metrics, up to
  bf16 noise.
- Text locality per edit is 4,096 tokens (819k tokens per method×layer cell).
- Bleed measures the *output* on other people. §6 (supplementary, 40 facts at L15) measures whether the unit's own activation stays low on other people's prompts: it does not.
- The two-hop setup differs from User-as-Engram's chains: here the second hop (city→country) is pretrained
  knowledge. That favours composition and makes the late-layer collapse more striking.

## 5. Multi-fact edits (10 / 50 facts per edit)

**Design.** The 200 main facts are split into 20 groups of 10, or 4 groups of 50. One edit stores a whole group at
one layer, in one of two forms:
- **shared**: a single unit, i.e. one rank-1 ΔW or one new neuron for all facts in the group;
- **per-fact**: n units, i.e. rank-n ΔW or n new neurons.

ROME becomes single-layer MEMIT (rank-n, λ = 1e4); qkv is rank-16 at every layer. Plain edits run at L7, L15 and
L23. +reg runs only at **L15**, per the lead's scope. Bleed here is the probability of *any* of the group's cities
on other people. Its no-edit baseline is higher than for single facts: 0.0044 for n=10 and 0.014 for n=50.
CIs are over edits (20 or 4 per cell).

**Findings.**
1. **One shared unit cannot hold many facts.**
   - With 50 facts in one shared rank-1 ΔW or one neuron, recall drops at L15/L23: lora_down 0.77 / 0.41, neuron
     0.74 / 0.54. Paraphrase falls to 0.05–0.19 and two-hop to ≈0.
   - Per-fact units keep recall at ≈1.0.
   - This is the expected capacity limit of a rank-1 / one-unit store. It also echoes AttMem's binding result: one
     carrier holds one item well and degrades fast after that.
2. **Separate gated neurons compose better than the equivalent rank-n LoRA.**
   - With one unit per fact at L7, n=10: neurons get para 0.41 / two-hop 0.31, about their single-fact level of
     0.44 / 0.29. Rank-10 down_proj LoRA gets 0.31 / 0.14, and rank-10 up_proj LoRA 0.32 / 0.19.
   - At n=50, L7: neurons 0.41 / 0.26 (neuron_bias 0.49 / 0.31) vs lora_down 0.34 / 0.17.
   - So the gate does matter once several facts share a layer. Separate units keep the facts from blurring into each
     other's directions, which a single-fact edit cannot show. This is the one place in T6 where "neuron" beats
     "rank-1 LoRA" on what the edit achieves rather than on sparsity.
3. **Counter-examples stop working when one edit carries many facts** (fixed 100-step budget).
   - +reg at L15 holds bleed down at n=10: neuron+reg 0.08 per-fact and 0.12 shared; lora_down+reg 0.07 and 0.10.
     Unregularised bleed is about 0.9.
   - At n=50, bleed is back to 0.44–0.62 for every +reg method.
   - Per-fact +reg neurons still fire on other people's prompts (97–98% above 10% of trigger), as in §6.
4. **Subject-keyed MEMIT scales best.** At L7, rank-10 / rank-50 MEMIT has bleed 0.028 / 0.12, para 0.57 / 0.57
   and two-hop 0.48 / 0.48, the same as a single-fact ROME. Its keys fire on other people only 2% of the time at
   L7, but 42–80% at L15 / L23.
5. **The late-layer collapse holds for multi-fact edits.** Two-hop at L23 is ≤0.04 for every trainable method, and
   MEMIT's falls to 0.31–0.35.
6. **The repo-style per-user LoRA (qkv r16, every layer) is the most contaminating.** It is 10.6M params per edit
   (about 13–860× more than the others). Bleed is 0.95 at both n, two-hop 0.03–0.09, and text KL 0.03–0.05, the
   largest of all. +reg lowers bleed to 0.22 at n=10 but only 0.80 at n=50.

## 6. Supplementary: does the unit fire on other people? (L15, 40 facts, +reg)

**Metric.** For each edit we run 40 *other* people's same-relation prompts ("<other> lives in the city of") and take
the unit's |activation| (max over positions) relative to its activation on the fact's own prompt (the trigger). For
rank-1 LoRA, the "unit" is the scalar projection x·a. Keys are `supp|…`, file `runs_supp.jsonl`.

| method (L15, +reg) | n | fires >10% of trigger on others | >50% | mean ratio | bleed | text fire10 | para | two-hop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| neuron+reg | 40 | 0.711 [0.577, 0.836] | 0.379 [0.266, 0.501] | 0.435 [0.323, 0.561] | 0.013 [0.006, 0.023] | 0.015 | 0.367 | 0.200 |
| lora_down+reg | 40 | 0.999 [0.996, 1.000] | 0.471 [0.351, 0.592] | 0.481 [0.430, 0.533] | 0.002 [0.001, 0.003] | 0.056 | 0.325 | 0.138 |

**Reading.**
- Counter-examples make the *output* subject-specific (bleed ≈ 0.01). They do **not** make the gated neuron fire only
  for its subject: it still reaches about half its trigger activation for most other people in the same frame. It is
  only modestly more selective than rank-1 LoRA, at 71% vs 100% of other people above 10%.
- Specificity is a soft threshold: other people's activation is too weak to move the answer. It is not an address.
  Subject-keyed edits (ROME) and Engram's hash lookup are near-zero off-subject by construction.
- The gate's distinguishing effect is still silence on *unrelated* text (§2.1), not selectivity among people.
- Caveat: the max is over all prompt positions, including the shared relation tokens. So this measures whether the
  unit is engaged anywhere in another person's prompt, not only at the name.
